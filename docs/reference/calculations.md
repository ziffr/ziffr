# The Calculation Catalogue

Every calculation Ziffr does on values, with its rule in plain words, the code that does it, the reference it is held to and the tests that hold it there (spec 0022, "Verification is the first requirement"). How the tests work, and how to add one, is in [the calculation tests guide](../guides/calculation-tests.md).

**What is checked.** `packages/core/src/catalogue.test.ts` fails when an exported function of a calculation module (`packages/core/calculation-modules.json`) has no row here, when a module has no section, and when a mutant that mutation testing leaves out has no line under "Mutants that cannot matter". So a new calculation cannot slip in untested.

**How to read a row.** _Code_ names the module and its functions. _Reference_ is a file in `packages/core/src/test/reference/`, written apart from the code and checked by its own hand-worked tests. _Tests_ are files in `packages/core/src/`. "Exact" means the double nearest to the exact decimal result, ties to the even one, never a floating-point neighbour.

**What every module gets.** Each calculation module is held to 100 % of lines and branches by coverage. Stryker mutation testing holds it to a score of at least 95 %.

## Derived Metrics

| Calculation | Rule | Code | Reference | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A formula | + − × ÷, brackets, unary minus, numbers such as 12 or 0.5, {slug}, and `prev({slug})` and `change({slug})`, which take one {slug} alone between their brackets: `prev` is that input's value in the period before, `change` is its value minus that one (so `change({x})` is `{x} - prev({x})`). ## Analyses and exports

| Calculation | Rule | Code | Reference | Tests |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basis choice | Two Metrics are compared on how each changed from the period before ("compared by change, growth removed") where both have a clear trend, else on their values ("compared by value"). A trend is clear when the least-squares line through the series by period position explains more than half of its variance: R² = Sxy² ÷ (Sxx · Syy) above 1/2, decided exactly as 2 · Sxy² > Sxx · Syy on fractions of the values' text; a series that does not change, or has fewer than two values, has none. A change is the exact difference of the values' text, as the nearest double, and only where the period before is there. `detrended` is each value minus its exact straight-line trend. A value that is not a number is no value; a period given twice counts once with the last value given. Tolerance: exact; against SciPy and NumPy to 1e-9 (leftovers) and 1e-12 (changes). | `src/basis.ts`: `changes`, `detrended`, `basisFor`, `basisText`, `readSeries` | `basis.ts` (`referenceChange`, `referenceLine`, `referenceBasis`), `fixtures/basis.py` for SciPy and NumPy | `basis.test.ts` |
| Suitability checks | Run by every Method on the series it will use, before it says anything (spec 0041, rule 12). **Enough periods**: the Method's minimum (correlation 6, lag 12, a fit 8 + 4 per driver, forecast 4, seasonal two seasons, change point 8, before a date 6, usual range 8) or a refusal with how many it has and needs. **Enough that vary**: at least 4 different values and at least half of the steps changing, else a refusal. **Gaps**: more than 1/5 of the window's periods without a value, or 3 in a row, is a refusal; fewer a caution naming how many were left out; a gap is never filled. **Far-off values**: a value more than 4 median absolute deviations from the median (median and deviation exact; a deviation of 0 flags nothing) and leaving out the 1 to 3 most extreme moves the result by more than 1/5 of its range, or flips its sign: a caution with the result without them; nothing is removed. **Skew and ties**: skewness above 2 (m3 ÷ m2^1.5 decided exactly as n · S3² > 4 · S2³) or more than half of the values repeating: ranks instead of values where the Method can, else a caution. **Not a straight line**: the misses ordered by the fitted value change sign too rarely, by the exact runs test (every arrangement of the signs counted) at 5 %, one-sided: a caution. **Changing relationship**: r of the first and last half of the pairs (at least 6 each): a flip with both |r| at 0.3 or more, or a drop below half from a link at 0.4 or more (or the reverse): a caution naming the latest half. **Level shifts**: the best split (4 or more values each side) of y = a + b·position + c·step, fitted exactly; a step of 3 typical misses or more, decided exactly as (n − 3) · c² ≥ 9 · SSE: the data after it where the Method can and its minimum remains, else a caution. **Seasonality that does not repeat**: the yearly patterns of the first and last half of the seasons differ by more than 1/2 of their combined size: a plain trend where the Method can, else a caution. **Spread that grows**: Spearman's rank correlation of level and size of miss above 1/2 (exact, ties share the mean rank): ranges as a share of the level where positive, else a caution. **Mixed units or periods**: Aggregation none beside another period, or an input built from another: a refusal naming them. **Too many Metrics**: more than one driver per 6 periods or 6: a first pass where the Method has one, else a refusal. Every threshold is in the table `SUITABILITY`, each tested one step either side. Skewness, median and deviation, Spearman's r, the normal 97.5 % point, the runs tail, the step fit and the yearly difference are checked against SciPy and NumPy to 1e-9 (the runs tail and the decisions exactly). | `src/suitability.ts`: `checkSuitability`, `verdictOutcome`, `minimumPeriods`, `driversAllowed`, `skewness`, `runsTail`, `levelShift`, `seasonDifference`, `rankAssociation`, `linkChange` | `suitability.ts` (`referenceMedian`, `referenceMad`, `referenceFarOff`, `referenceSkewSquared`, `referenceRunsTail`, `referenceSpearman`, `referenceStep`, `referenceSeasonDifference`, `referenceGaps`, `referenceTooFlat`), `fixtures/suitability.py` for SciPy and NumPy | `suitability.test.ts` |
| Strength words | A result is strong above |r| 0.7, moderate above 0.4, weak above 0.2, else "can't tell from chance"; and it is "can't tell from chance" too where |r| does not clear chance for n pairs: atanh(|r|) · √(n − 3) below 1.959963984540054 (the normal curve's 97.5 % point, as SciPy gives it), or n at 3 or fewer. A result with a caution is never strong (strong becomes moderate). The thresholds are one table, `STRENGTH`, which `strength` in the correlation module reads too. Where the best of several results was picked (the best of up to 7 lags), the bar is the normal curve's 1 − 0.025 ÷ looks point (`CHANCE_BY_TRIES`, SciPy's `norm.ppf`: 1.960, 2.241, 2.394, 2.498, 2.576, 2.638, 2.690), so a best-of-seven has to clear more than one look. | Takeaway wording | Every sentence, caution, refusal and label is data keyed by case (English so far), written through the Workspace's Number Style and each Metric's unit by `formatNumber` and `formatValue`; the one footer line is "This shows what has gone together, not what caused what." | Correlation | Pearson's r on the periods both Metrics have, at least six of them, on the basis above (the changes where both trend, which leaves one fewer period; "values" when asked for). The shorter period is combined to the longer, complete periods only. r² = Sxy² ÷ (Sxx · Syy) is exact, and r is ±√ of its nearest double. A series that does not change has none. ## The Methods library (spec 0041)

## Mutants that cannot matter

Mutation testing changes the code on purpose and expects a test to fail. A change that leaves every result the same cannot fail a test. Such a mutant is left out in the code with a `// Stryker disable next-line <mutator>: <reason>` comment, and listed here as `file` · mutator · reason. The catalogue test fails when a comment has no line here.
