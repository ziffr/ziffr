# Setup trial materials: what a person is given

Give each person only the scenario card for their profile, one at a time, printed or on screen. Do not give them the protocol's tables or the intended readings. The protocol is [the trial file](2026-11-setup-guide-trial.md).

## The words to say first

> We are testing Ziffr, not you. Nothing you do is wrong. Please say your thoughts aloud as you go. I will not help, so if something is unclear, that is exactly what we want to hear. You can stop or skip anything, any time. I will record the screen and your voice if you agree, and I will not write down your name. Ziffr will send real messages to your own email address during this session; nothing goes to anyone else.

Ask for a yes to the recording and note it. Never record without one.

## Scenario cards

### Card 1. The solo founder

You have started a small software company. You sell a tool to other businesses, and you work mostly alone. You are told to keep track of a few numbers (money coming in, customers, how many leave) and to have them ready for an investor. Someone told you to try Ziffr. Sign in with your work email address and set it up the way you would, for yourself. You may stop when you feel you are done.

### Card 2. The office manager

You work in a design agency with about 20 people. You run the office and, because nobody else does, the numbers: hours billed, invoices open, how busy everyone is. The owner of the agency wants a monthly overview. A colleague told you about Ziffr. Sign in with your work email address and set it up the way you would for the agency. You may stop when you feel you are done.

### Card 3. The head of finance

You lead finance at a healthcare company of about 200 people. Every month you collect numbers from many people and put them in a report for the management and, four times a year, the board. You are looking for a tool that asks people for their numbers instead of you chasing them. You have been told Ziffr can do that. Sign in with your work email address and set it up as you would for your team. You may stop when you feel you are done.

### Card 4. The IT admin who runs Ziffr

Your company asked you to run Ziffr on your own server, for the finance team, who will use it. You have installed it. Your company does not use Slack. You have set up a mail server. Open Ziffr, sign in with your work email address and create the first Workspace as you would for the finance team. You may stop when you feel you are done.

For the owner: use a fresh Ziffr with a mail server and no Slack app, and keep the person's address as the Owner.

### Card 5. The team member

A colleague, who runs the numbers for your team, has invited you to a tool called Ziffr. You got an email. Open it and do what you would do. You may stop when you feel you are done.

For the owner: the colleague is the owner of the trial Workspace, which has one Metric with a value. Send the invitation to the person's own address and say nothing more.

## What the person may ask

The only reply is "What do you think it means?" or "What would you do?". If they ask whether a step is required, say "You decide." If they ask whether it is safe, say "What would you want to know?" and write down what they say.

## The test message

Write down, for every Send me a test and every Ask me now: the way, the minute it was pressed, the minute the person said it arrived (or "never"), and what the screen said. A message that does not arrive within 5 minutes is a finding even when the person did not mind.

## Closing questions, to read out

1. In your own words, what is Ziffr for?
2. What does "Ways in and out" mean to you? What would you expect to find there?
3. Which way would you use to send in a number? Which would you use to be asked? Why?
4. Ziffr suggested some Packs. What is a Pack? Would you have picked the one at the top? Why or why not?
5. Where do the answers you gave about your business go?
6. Who can see what you said you do?
7. If you came back tomorrow, where would you find these steps again?
8. What would you do next?
9. What was hardest? What would you tell a colleague?

## After the session

Within the hour, before talking to anyone: finish the observation sheet from the recording, fill in the person's sheet in the protocol, and write down the three moments where the person's face or voice changed. Then fill the tables in the protocol and apply the rule: anything misread or abandoned by two of five becomes a ticket.
