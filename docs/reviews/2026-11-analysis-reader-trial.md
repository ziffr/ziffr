# Reader trial: Takeaways, the method picker and the guides (spec 0041, ticket 17)

**The trial with five people is still to be done by the owner; the simulated pre-read only removes the worst wording before it.**

This is the kit for that trial and the record of the pre-read. The ticket (#710) is done when the owner has run the trial, filled in the tables below and reworded what two of five misread. The wording of every item is in [the materials file](2026-11-analysis-reader-trial-materials.md), printed by the code that writes it.

## What the trial answers

Spec 0041 promises that a person who never studied statistics reads a Takeaway the way it was meant: no number taken for a promise, no "moves with" taken for "causes", no "can't tell from chance" taken for "no link". Tests can check that the forbidden words are absent. Only people can say what the words mean to them. The trial finds the lines that two or more of five people read wrongly, and those lines are reworded.

## Who qualifies

Five people. Each one:

- uses numbers at work every week (sales, takings, orders, tickets, costs: in a spreadsheet, a report or a dashboard), and decides something from them;
- has never studied statistics beyond school, and does not work as an analyst, data scientist or in a finance planning role that builds forecasts;
- has never used Ziffr and has not read its documents;
- is not a friend or colleague of the person running the session (they must feel free to say "I don't get it").

Spread the five over different jobs: for instance a shop or small business owner, someone in operations, in marketing, in finance administration and in a support team. Do not take two from the same job. Note first name, job and how often they work with numbers; nothing else. Do not record names in this file.

## The session (45 minutes, one person at a time)

| Minutes | What |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 5 | Welcome. Say: "We are testing the texts, not you. Nothing you say is wrong. Please say your thoughts aloud as you read. I will not explain anything, so if something is unclear, that is exactly what we want to hear." Show the one-paragraph introduction at the top of the materials ("What Ziffr shows"). |
| 5 to 38 | The core items (below), one at a time, printed or on screen, in the order of the list. For each item ask the four questions. About 80 seconds an item. |
| 38 to 43 | The guide: give them page one of [Reading a Takeaway](../guides/reading-a-takeaway.md) (down to "A caution"), and ask the guide questions. |
| 43 to 45 | "Which words or lines were hardest?" Thank them. |

**Rules for whoever runs it.** No coaching: never say "so it means...", never nod at a right answer or frown at a wrong one, never answer a question about a word ("What do you think it means?" is the only reply). Do not explain, even at the end, until the score sheet is filled in. Think aloud is encouraged; if they go quiet for more than 10 seconds say "What are you thinking?". Record the session (with consent) or write the answers down word for word; the scorers read the words, not your impression. Sit beside the person, not opposite, and do not look at the paper while they read.

### The core items

All five read the same 24 items, in this order: **R1, R2, R4, R5, R6, R7, R9, R10, R11, P2, P4, P7, S1, S2, S4, S5, H1, H3, H5, C1, C2, C3, C7, C9**. If a reader is fast, continue with the reserve items (all others, in file order). R3, R8, P1, P3, P5, P6, S3, S6, H2, H4 and C4, C5, C6, C8, C10 to C15 are the reserve; they repeat what the core items test with other words.

## The four questions

For an item in Part A or B (a Takeaway or a Heads-up):

1. **In your own words, what does this say?**
2. **What would you do next?**
3. **What does it NOT tell you?**
4. **Is any word or line unclear?** Which one, and what did you think it meant?

For an item in Part C (the list under Choose a method):

1. **In your own words, what does this choice do?**
2. **When would you pick it, and when would you not?**
3. **What would you expect it to do that it may not?**
4. **Is any word unclear?** Which one?

For the guide (Reading a Takeaway, page one), after reading it: **What does "can't tell from chance" mean? What should you do when you see it? What does a caution line mean? What does Ziffr mean when it refuses? Why does it never say that one number causes another?**

## Scoring

1. Two scorers read the written answers independently, mark each reader and item **M** (misread), **U** (a word or line named as unclear, with the word), **O** (read as intended and nothing flagged), and then settle differences by talking. A third person decides a tie.
2. **An item is reworded when two or more of the five readers misread it.** That is the owner's rule from the ticket.
3. **An item is also reworded when three or more of five flag the same word or line as unclear and a plainer word exists**, even if they guessed the meaning. A guess that happens to be right is luck.
4. An item that one reader misreads is not reworded but goes to the **watch list**: look at it again after the next round.
5. A word or line that is unclear to everyone but is a term the user guide explains (Target Range, Breakdown) is noted, not reworded: the trial gives no context for it, a real person has it.
6. After rewording, the changed items go to two new readers (retest). A reworded item that one of the two misreads goes back to step 3.
7. Never reword by hand around the code's rules: the footer of each kind, the strength words and the forbidden words stay (`takeaways/wording.ts` is the one place; its tests say what is allowed).

## Simulated pre-read (not people)

**This is not a substitute for the five people.** Five fresh Sonnet agents, each given only the printed materials and the questions, none with the repository or any explanation, answered as a non-analyst business user: a shop owner, an operations manager, a marketing lead, a finance clerk and a support team lead. They answered 43 of the 44 items (the core and the reserve), in the order of the file; the Heads-up about a Target (H4) was not in the file they were given, because the script that printed it left it out, so no simulated reader saw it. They read generously and in a different way to a tired person in a hurry, they never get bored or skip a line, and the same model wrote some of the words it read, so the pre-read finds what is plainly confusing and misses what is subtly misread. What it can do is remove the worst before real people lose their time on it.

The answers were scored with the rules above by the person who wrote the pre-read, so a second scorer may mark differently.

### Not changed, and why

- **"Compared by change, growth removed"** is the basis line on most Takeaways. All five simulated readers called it unclear and all five guessed it right. Changing it touches the basis of every Takeaway and the Show the numbers of the Metric page; it goes to the real readers first.
- **"Can't tell from chance"** (S1): see the watch list above.
- **The Heads-up "Visits's"**, and the plural verbs in Heads-ups ("when Support tickets rises"): the sentence takes the Metric's name as written. Worth a decision, not a rewording.

### What the pre-read cannot say

It cannot say what a person who is in a hurry skips. It cannot say whether the footer is read at all. It says nothing about the charts, which the trial leaves out on purpose. The five readers were one model in five costumes: where they agree, check it with people; where they all find a word hard, it is hard.
