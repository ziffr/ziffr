# Setup trial: five people set up a Workspace with no help (spec 0052, ticket 14)

**The trial with five people is still to be done by the owner. Nothing here has been run with people.**

This is the kit for ticket 14 and the place to record it. The ticket is done when the owner has run the trial, filled in the tables below, filed the tickets the rule asks for and written the findings into the spec's Further Notes. What each person is given, and the sheets to print, are in [the materials file](2026-11-setup-guide-trial-materials.md).

## What the trial answers

Spec 0052 promises that a person new to Ziffr gets from the first sign-in to a working loop (a number asked for and a number sent in) and to a team, without help and without feeling pushed. Tests check the ticks and the rules. Only people can say whether the words and the order make sense. The trial answers:

1. Where do people hesitate, stop or give up?
2. What do they skip, and why?
3. Do they understand **Ways in and out**: what comes in, what goes out, and that the page is about how numbers reach Ziffr and how people are asked?
4. Is the Pack Ziffr suggests the one they would have picked?
5. Does the first test arrive (the Test Message, and **Ask me now** with the value coming back)?
6. Do they invite someone, and do they know whom they would ask for what?
7. Do they read the privacy line and believe it?

## Who qualifies

Five people, one of each profile below. Each has never used Ziffr, has not read its documents and is not a friend or colleague of the person running the session (they must feel free to say "I don't get it"). Use each person's own work email: Ziffr sends real messages by email, and Slack and Telegram only where this Ziffr has them. Never use a made-up address.

| # | Profile | Plays | Where | What it checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **A solo founder** of a small software company that sells to businesses | The Owner of a new Workspace | A trial on hosted Ziffr | The shortest path; whether one person alone finishes; the SaaS metrics Pack suggested first (industry, whom it sells to and the function Management match) |
| 2 | **An office manager at a 20-person agency** who also looks after the numbers | The Owner | A trial on hosted Ziffr | Plain words for a person who is not technical; the Agency or freelancer Pack (industry agency, 11 to 50 people); Slack as a way |
| 3 | **A head of finance at a company of about 200 people** | The Owner | A trial on hosted Ziffr | Whether a careful person trusts the privacy line; the Team step and **Who is asked for what**; Finance and Board meeting deck among the suggestions (function Finance) |
| 4 | **An IT admin who runs Ziffr for their company, and whose company does not use Slack** | The Owner of the first Workspace on a Ziffr of their own | A fresh self-hosted Ziffr (Docker or the local stack), with a mail server set up and no Slack app | The words for a missing way ("This Ziffr has no Slack app yet"); that the guide still finishes with the form and paste; where the Installation Admin is sent |
| 5 | **A team member invited by a colleague** | A Member joining an existing Workspace | A Workspace the owner set up, with the person invited by email | The short version (**About you** and **Ways in and out** only); whether it feels like a welcome or a chore; that they are never shown the Owner's setup |

Spread the five over different jobs; do not take two from the same one. Note first name, job and how often they use numbers; nothing else. Do not record names in this file.

## Before each session

- A new Workspace each time (profile 5 joins one the owner made, with a Metric and one value already there). The demo is no use: it has no guide.
- Check what this Ziffr can send: for profiles 1 to 3 email works, Slack and Telegram work only if the hosted Ziffr has them. Write down which ways were real, because it changes what the person can try.
- Start the recording (with consent) or have a second person take notes. The notes are the person's words, not your impression.
- Turn off anything that helps: no second window with the user guide, no chat with the owner.

## The session (40 minutes, one person at a time)

| Minutes | What |
| --- | --- |
| 0 to 5 | Welcome. Say: "We are testing Ziffr, not you. Nothing you do is wrong. Please say your thoughts aloud. I will not help, so if something is unclear, that is exactly what we want to hear." Give the scenario card for the profile (materials file). Do not say what Ziffr is. |
| 5 to 30 | The person signs in (or opens the invitation) and sets up the Workspace as they like. Do not name the steps. Watch (below). They may stop whenever they would stop. |
| 30 to 36 | The questions after (materials file): **Ways in and out**, the Pack, the privacy line, the team. |
| 36 to 40 | "What was hardest? What would you tell a colleague?" Thank them. |

**Rules for whoever runs it.** No coaching: never say "so it means...", never point at a button, never answer a question about a word ("What do you think it means?" is the only reply). If they go quiet for more than 10 seconds, say "What are you thinking?". If they are stuck for two minutes, write down where and why, then say "You may skip that" or "Go on as you would", and mark the step **helped**. Do not explain anything until the score sheet is filled in.

## What to watch

For each person, mark each step of the guide with one letter, and note the minute and the person's words:

| Mark | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **D** | Done: the step ticked, or the person said they were done |
| **S** | Skipped on purpose (with their reason) |
| **A** | Abandoned: left it open and went elsewhere, or closed it |
| **H** | Hesitated: more than 30 seconds without acting, or went back, or read the same text twice |
| **M** | Misread: said something that matches a wrong reading below |
| **E** | Needed help (see the rules) |

Watch for these in particular:

- **The start.** Do they notice that Ziffr opened a guide by itself? Do they use **Skip for now** or **Go to Ziffr** at once? Do they find the list of steps again later (the Setup line, Help, their menu)?
- **About you.** Which questions they leave empty and why. Whether they read the line under each question and the privacy line; whether they believe "stays in this Workspace". Whether they understand that the function question is about them, not the company.
- **Starting point.** Do they choose **Explore on my own** or **Start from a Pack**? Is the first suggestion the one they would pick (profile 1 expects the SaaS metrics Pack, profile 2 the Agency or freelancer Pack, profile 3 the Finance Pack or the Board meeting deck)? Do they open **Preview**? Do they understand it shows Sample Data and not their numbers? Do they find **Set up from this Pack** and tick or untick a Metric?
- **Ways in and out.** Do they understand that the first question is about numbers coming **in** and the second about people being asked and told? Which card they try first. Do they press **Send me a test**? Does the test arrive, and how long do they wait? Do they understand a way that is missing here (profile 4)? Do they see **More ways**, and do they ask what it is?
- **First Metric.** Do they press **Ask me now**? Do they find the message where it says it went? Do they answer it, use the form link or paste? Do they understand "waiting for your value"? Do they open **Didn't arrive?** when something is late?
- **Team.** Do they invite someone? By email or by link? Do they read the lines about the four roles? Do they know whom they would ask for which Metric? Do they turn on Domain Joining, and do they understand it?
- **Afterwards.** Do they go on to dashboards, or stop at the end of the guide? Do they know what to do next?
- **Profile 5 (a Member).** Do they understand why they are seeing only two steps? Do they feel they were given work? Do they set how they are asked?

## Questions after the session

Ask these in order, write the answers down word for word, and do not correct them.

1. **In your own words, what is Ziffr for?**
2. **What does "Ways in and out" mean to you? What would you expect to find there?** (Intended: how numbers reach Ziffr, and how people are asked and told.)
3. **Which way would you use to send in a number? Which would you use to be asked?** Why?
4. **Ziffr suggested some Packs. What is a Pack? Would you have picked the one at the top? Why or why not?** (Intended: ready-made Metrics, a dashboard and a Workflow to start from, which can be changed; nothing is installed until you press.)
5. **Where do the answers you gave about your business go?** (Intended: nowhere outside this Workspace.)
6. **Who can see what you said you do?** (Intended, for a Member: the Owners and Admins; for an Owner: the person and the Owners and Admins.)
7. **If you came back tomorrow, where would you find these steps again?** (Intended: the line at the top, **Setup Guide** in Help or the menu.)
8. **What would you do next?**

Misreads for scoring (the readers never see this table):

| Question | Intended reading | Common wrong readings |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | How numbers reach Ziffr, and how people are asked and told | Read as "connections to other software only"; as "who can log in"; as "notifications" |
| 4 | A starting set the person changes, installed only on a press | Read as an app or plugin that runs by itself; as something already installed; as a template that cannot be changed |
| 5 | Stays in the Workspace, sent nowhere | Read as "sent to Ziffr to improve the product"; as visible to every member |
| 6 | The person and the Owners and Admins see a function | Read as "everyone sees it"; as "nobody sees it" |
| 7 | The Setup line, Help, or the person's menu | "I would have to start again"; "I can't" |

## Scoring

1. Two people read the notes independently and mark each person and step **D, S, A, H, M** or **E**, then settle differences by talking. A third person decides a tie.
2. **A step, a word or a screen is changed when two or more of the five misread it or abandon it.** That is the owner's rule from the ticket: anything misread or abandoned by two of five becomes a ticket.
3. **A step is also looked at when three or more of five hesitate at the same place**, even if they get through.
4. Something one person misreads or abandons goes on the **watch list**: look at it again after the next change.
5. A person who needed help (**E**) counts as abandoned for that step.
6. Where the profile explains it (profile 4 meets a missing Slack app on purpose; profile 5 sees two steps on purpose), the intended result is not a finding.
7. Never change the guide's rules to fit a finding without the owner: the privacy line, who sees what and the limits stay (`docs/security/threat-model.md`, entries 27 and 28).
