# Scheduled jobs

Things in Ziffr happen on a clock, and something outside Ziffr has to call them: your platform's scheduler, a cron line on the server, or any service that can make an HTTP request. They all take the same secret and are safe to call as often as you like.

```sh skip="a setting for the server"
ZIFFR_JOB_SECRET=$(openssl rand -hex 32)   # at least 32 characters; without it every job is off
```

| Job | Call it | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `POST /api/jobs/workflows` | every hour | Runs each workspace's Workflows once a day, at its run hour in its own time zone: asks for missing values, reminds before the Due Date, escalates after it, reminds people of tasks due within two days ([spec 0005](../specs/0005-workflows-on-a-clock.md)), and takes the Snapshots of dashboards set to take them by themselves once their period's Metrics are Locked or their wait is over ([spec 0013](../specs/0013-dashboard-snapshots.md)) |
| `POST /api/jobs/heads-ups` | every hour | Looks through each Workspace's Metrics (the 200 most recently updated that are in use, never the demo) for pairs whose changes have started or stopped moving together, and keeps what it finds in the pair table. A Workspace is scanned at most every six hours, however many calls come, also from two servers at once; rows a scan did not refresh, and rows older than 90 days, are deleted. The pair table is derived from values, read by nothing but the core, and never by a browser, a Connector or a View Link: a person's Heads-ups are worked out from the rows of Metrics they may see ([spec 0041](../specs/0041-analysis-in-plain-words.md)). A call starts no new scan after eight minutes; the Workspaces it did not reach (`left`) wait for the next call. Answers counts only: `workspaces`, `scanned`, `skipped`, `failed`, `pairs`, `expired`, `left`, `told`. After a Workspace is scanned the same call also makes the Heads-up messages of the people who asked for them (`told` counts the messages made; see [Heads-ups](#heads-ups)). Docker Compose calls it at ten past every hour. See [Heads-ups](#heads-ups) |
| `POST /api/jobs/demo-reset` | once a night, only for a public demo | Resets the demo workspace, as its reset button does, so a demo anyone may try starts each day clean. Answers `{ "reset": false }` when a visitor reset it less than a minute before, and 404 where the demo is off. Docker Compose calls it at 03:30 UTC when `ZIFFR_DEMO_ONLY=1` |
| `POST /api/jobs/trial-cleanup` | every hour, only where trials are offered | Reminds the Owners of Trial Workspaces that end within a day (once each), closes those that ended (revokes their keys, disconnects Slack and Telegram) and deletes those that ended a week ago with everything in them, and their Owners when it was their only workspace (spec 0021). Answers counts only, and 404 where trials are off. Docker Compose calls it at twenty past every hour when `ZIFFR_TRIAL_DAYS` is set |
| `POST /api/jobs/feedback` | every five minutes, hosted Ziffr only | Files up to five open Feedback on GitHub through the Ziffr bot (an issue labelled `feedback` for a problem, a Discussion for an idea or a question), never naming the person, and records the post's number and link. A feedback is tried at most five times, ten minutes apart; a retry first looks for the post by its reference, so it is never filed twice. Needs `ZIFFR_FEEDBACK_GITHUB_TOKEN`, `ZIFFR_FEEDBACK_GITHUB_REPO` (`owner/name`) and `ZIFFR_FEEDBACK_GITHUB_CATEGORY_ID`; answers 503 naming the missing ones, 404 off hosted Ziffr, counts only otherwise ([spec 0049](../specs/0049-feedback.md)) |
| `POST /api/jobs/unused-workspaces` | daily | Where `ZIFFR_DELETE_UNUSED_AFTER_DAYS` and a default Workspace Limit are set: warns the Owners of Workspaces on the default limit unused for that many days (once each) and deletes those still unused 14 days after their warning, key first (spec 0029). Answers counts only, and does nothing where the setting is off. Docker Compose calls it at 04:10 UTC |
| `POST /api/jobs/usage-digest` | daily | On days 1 to 7 of a month, queues the monthly usage email to each Owner of a Workspace that has something unused (not trials, not the demo, not within 90 days of counting), once per Owner and month; the deliveries job sends it. Does nothing without working email or without `ZIFFR_SECRETS_KEY` or `ZIFFR_JOB_SECRET` to sign its unsubscribe link. Answers counts only. Docker Compose calls it at 07:00 UTC (spec 0031) |
| `POST /api/jobs/workspace-keys` | every five minutes | Gives every Workspace a Workspace Key, wraps keys again under `ZIFFR_SECRETS_KEY` after it was changed (`rewrapped`, `rewrapFailed` for ones it could not open, `rewrapLeft`), seals what was written before the upgrade and moves what is sealed under an older key version to the current one, then destroys a retired key version once nothing refers to it (`keysDestroyed`): a few hundred rows at a time, for up to 45 seconds per call, so what is left waits for the next call. It can stop and start at any time and is safe to call twice at once. Answers counts only, and `waiting: true` while there is no `ZIFFR_SECRETS_KEY`. How far it is shows on the Installation settings page (spec 0028). Docker Compose calls it at two past every fifth minute |
| `POST /api/jobs/retention` | daily | Lets confirmed Retentions start on their day (and sends the reminder the day before), then condenses or deletes the old values of every Metric with a Retention in force, in small steps: at most 5,000 original values and 60 seconds for each Metric a day, carrying on the next day. It takes no more than the counts a person confirmed, checks every Condensed Value against the original values before deleting them, and leaves periods that open work holds. Each Metric runs once a day however many calls come, also from two servers at once. Answers counts only. Docker Compose calls it at 02:40 UTC. See [Retention](#retention) |
| `POST /api/jobs/deliveries` | every few minutes | Sends emails, Slack and Telegram messages that are waiting: the day's Prompts, reminders and Escalations from the Workflow run, reminders and requests for missing values sent from the Connectors page, retries after a failure, and ones held back by the sending limits ([email](email.md), [Slack](slack.md)) |

```sh skip="a crontab line with the job secret, which the test app does not set"
# crontab, for example
0 * * * *    curl -fsS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $ZIFFR_JOB_SECRET" https://ziffr.example.com/api/jobs/workflows
10 * * * *   curl -fsS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $ZIFFR_JOB_SECRET" https://ziffr.example.com/api/jobs/heads-ups
*/5 * * * *  curl -fsS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $ZIFFR_JOB_SECRET" https://ziffr.example.com/api/jobs/deliveries
*/5 * * * *  curl -fsS -X POST -H "Authorization: Bearer $ZIFFR_JOB_SECRET" https://ziffr.example.com/api/jobs/workspace-keys
```

Without the secret, or with a wrong one, a job answers 404. With it, a job answers only counts: how many workspaces ran or failed, how many emails and Slack messages were sent or failed.

## The day's run

Owners and Admins set the workspace's time zone and the hour its Workflows run on the Team page (UTC and 08:00 until they do). The first call after that hour runs the day; every later call that day does nothing for that workspace, also when two servers call at once. A workspace whose run fails is marked so and the others go on; the next day runs as usual. The demo is never run by the clock.

## Retention

The Retention job (spec 0033) works through every Metric that has a Retention in force, and does nothing where none has. It is safe to call as often as you like and from several servers; what you need to know as the person who runs Ziffr:

- **Claims.** A Metric's day is claimed in the `retention_runs` table before any work starts, so two calls the same day do the work once. A run that was stopped (a restart, a crash) is taken over by the next call after ten minutes and carries on from what it left: a span half-way is checked again, never trusted.
- **Steps.** A first run over ten years of daily values takes days by design: each Metric gets at most 5,000 original values and a minute a day. Call it daily; calling it more often does not speed it up.
- **Held to the confirmation.** A Retention in force carries the counts its Owner confirmed (`retention_cap`). The periods and values earlier runs took count against it (`retention_runs.confirmation`), so values that arrive for periods already cleared cannot make room for more. If a Metric then holds more old periods or values than that, the job does nothing for it until someone confirms again (on its page, the same setting again, after looking at the counts), and says so on the Metric's page and in the Audit Log. A Retention without a confirmation on record is never run.
- **Failures.** A failed run is rolled back (a span is condensed, checked and deleted in one step; the delete is one database transaction) and tried the next day. After three failures in a row the Metric stops and shows its Owners why; the server log has the error's code, never a value. Confirming the Retention again starts it clean.
- **Backups.** Deleting a value deletes it from Ziffr. Copies in your backups remain for as long as you keep backups; hosted Ziffr keeps them for 30 days. Only deleting a whole Workspace and destroying its key makes data unreadable in backups.
- **The demo** is never touched.

## Heads-ups

The Heads-up job (spec 0041) fills the **pair table**, which holds for every pair of a Workspace's Metrics that can be compared how their changes correlate. What you need to know as the person who runs Ziffr:

- **What it looks at.** The 200 most recently updated Metrics that are in use (not archived, showing values), the demo never. Two Metrics are compared on the longer of their periods, and only where the periods are the same or one step apart (week and month, not week and quarter). The table keeps every pair; a Derived Metric is left out of a person's Heads-ups with a Metric it is worked out from, or with another worked out from the same, judged over the Metrics that person sees. The latest 120 periods of each are used; a pair whose latest period is more than 6 periods before today is not current and is left out. At most 20,000 pairs are looked at. The scan also has one work allowance for all its exact arithmetic (about seven seconds of a laptop under load), taken in the order of the Metrics' ids: once it is spent, a pair that would need exact work is not scanned, while a pair that needs none still is, so the pairs scanned are a subset of what a full scan scans and not the first ones in id order. Pairs not scanned are never a Heads-up, are not counted as looked at (the Takeaway's numbers say how many were not scanned), and count as tests of chance 1 in the correction, so the bar for the others is never lower; the scan is then recorded as a capped one.
- **More than 200 Metrics.** The scan covers the 200 most recently updated, and says so (`capped`). So that nobody's Heads-ups depend on Metrics they may not see, a person in such a Workspace has their own pairs scanned when they ask, over the Metrics they see (the same 200 limit), instead of read from the table; it takes a few seconds. **Security note (accepted):** members can infer "more than 200 Metrics are in use in this Workspace" from how long and how fresh their answer is. No name, value, correction or Heads-up of a Metric they may not see is affected. While a scan replaces the rows, the flag only gets stricter (it says capped until the rows are all written), so a reader never takes the table's rows for a capped scan.
- **One scan is bounded.** A scan reads at most 10 years of values and 19,900 pairs, which takes seconds, so the job checks its eight-minute budget between Workspaces only. A call killed part-way (the clock aborts after ten minutes) leaves a safe state: the Workspace's claim stands for six hours, the old rows stand, the capped flag is never loosened before the rows are complete, and the next scan starts clean.
- **A person's own scan has a budget.** In a capped Workspace the Heads-ups list scans a person's own Metrics (up to 200). The core keeps the finished Heads-ups (kilobytes; the pair rows are about 640 bytes each, measured, 12.7 MB for 19,900, and are never kept) for ten minutes for exactly the signature the person has now: the Metrics they see and the names, slugs, formulas and number style the words are made from, so a Metric hidden, shown, renamed or changed since is never answered from an older result. At most 100 results are kept per Workspace and 2,000 in all, so one Workspace cannot push another's out. A person may start six such scans an hour; over that, a result at most an hour old for the same signature answers, else the list says "could not be read" and tells nothing. Two scans run at a time for people at a page and one for the job, in a lane of its own; up to 50 wait their turn. The job reads what is kept but keeps nothing. The budget is in memory: a restart or a second server starts with none.
- **Messages.** After a scan, each person who switched on Heads-ups by message (and has them shown) gets the strongest ones they were not told yet, at most 3 in any 7 days, each once until it has ended and returned. They go through the same path as an Alert (the person's Delivery Preference for updates, Quiet hours, the Outbox, the send limits of the connector). Sight of every Metric of a Heads-up is asked again just before the message is made and again when it leaves; a person without it gets nothing and no trace of one. A message leaves only while the Heads-up it was made for still stands, and one that fails for good (no address, opted out, not set up, attempts used up, refused as it leaves) gives its place in the week back by its own row. People are taken in the order of whom the job looked at longest ago, at most 200 per Workspace per call and within the call's time budget. Nothing is sent from the demo. The log says only that a person's messages failed, never whose or what. The tables of a person's choices (`heads_up_settings`, `heads_up_dismissed`, `heads_up_mutes`, `heads_up_told`) are read by the core alone, like the pair table; a key holds Metric ids only.
- **Values used.** Only values that are final: a period whose value waits for Approval is left out of the scan until it is approved. A Derived Metric's values are those the app shows for it, worked out from its inputs whether or not the person sees them.
- **How often.** A Workspace is scanned at most every six hours (`heads_up_scans` claims the scan, so two calls at once do it once). A scan that fails leaves the old rows as they are and is counted under `failed`; the server log says only that a scan failed, never which Workspace or what it held.
- **Retention.** Each scan replaces the Workspace's rows: a pair that can no longer be compared, or a Metric no longer in use, is gone after the next scan. Rows no scan has refreshed for 90 days are deleted by the job. Deleting a Metric or a Workspace deletes its rows.
- **Who can read it.** Nobody but the core, with the service key: row level security is on and there is no policy, and the grants of the public roles are taken away. A person's Heads-ups read only the rows whose two Metrics they may see, and the correction for the number of pairs counts only those rows; a Metric that is archived or hidden disappears from their Heads-ups at once, without a new scan.
- **Measure before you size it.** A scan of 150 Metrics takes a few seconds on a laptop; the work grows with the square of the number of Metrics, which is why 200 is the limit.
